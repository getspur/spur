## On what SPUR actually is

> "The honest description is that SPUR is a distributed-systems kernel for agent execution that happens to look like a terminal app. The panels are the easy part. The hard part is the event-sourced lineage, the durable plan store, and making sure that when you close your laptop, nothing is lost."

---

## On why the cost ledger matters

> "Every vendor can tell you what you spent with them. None of them can tell you what you spent in total. That's not a feature gap they can close — it's a structural fact of who owns the data. SPUR sits above all of them, so it can. That's the one thing in the product that nobody else can ship."

---

## On the rate-limit problem we hear from Claude Code Max users

> "The pattern we kept hearing was the same: 'I pay $200 a month, I'm out by Wednesday, and I have no way to keep working without re-explaining everything to a different model.' SPUR doesn't fix the rate limit. It makes the rate limit a routing problem instead of a wall."

---

## On the deliberate decision to keep humans in the loop

> "We are explicitly not building a system that runs unattended overnight and merges its own code in the morning. There are other tools for that. SPUR treats review as a state machine, not a UI afterthought, because the developers we're building for don't actually want their name on a PR they didn't read."

---

## On being proprietary in a developer-tools world that defaults to open-source

> "We thought hard about open-core and decided against it for the orchestration kernel. The choice we made is to keep the core proprietary and the Community tier genuinely useful — one brain, one worker, full review loop, full cost display. If that doesn't feel generous enough in practice, that's a signal we have to keep listening for, not a position to defend."
