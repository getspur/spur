# SPUR Launch Video Production Brief

## Intent

Present the SPUR TUI as the main place where a person works directly with a
brain agent. The brain turns intent into work, delegates through Plan, Loop, or
Ad hoc execution, observes every worker in the control tower, and reviews
routine completions before escalating only critical decisions to the human.

The launch film should feel like a human-made product documentary captured from
the current implementation. It should not invent futuristic dashboards or use
generic AI imagery.

## Visual direction

- Warm terminal documentary: graphite and ink surfaces, restrained amber and
  green accents, real monospace UI, patient editorial rhythm.
- Use current TUI geometry and labels from Session Detail and Dashboard.
- Treat Plan, Loop, and Ad hoc as execution patterns visible through real state,
  not as invented permanent tabs.
- Prefer screen detail, cursor movement, state changes, and review evidence over
  decorative motion graphics.
- Use short authored text only when the product capture cannot carry the idea.

## Voiceover

Running more coding agents should not mean running more terminals. In SPUR, you
work directly with one brain. It turns intent into a plan, delegates bounded
work to the right specialists, and keeps every worker visible in one TUI.
Plan for dependent work. Loop for recurring goals. Delegate ad hoc when the
task is small. Each worker reports progress, cost, evidence, and lineage back
to the control tower. The brain reviews routine completions first. When the
evidence is clear, work moves forward. When a decision is critical, SPUR brings
it back to you with the diff, the context, and explicit choices. One
conversation above the work. One control surface across the agents. Talk to
the brain. Watch the work. SPUR.
